What you learn

Every month has a code. Add that code, the day of the month, and a code for the year, then take the remainder after dividing by seven. That number is the weekday — addition and one division you can finish while someone is still asking the question.

  1. Day numbers — 0 = Sunday through 6 = Saturday
  2. Take out the sevens — collapsing any total into a weekday number
  3. Month codes — twelve values, each with a memory hook
  4. Combine — your first real weekday
  5. Year code — turn any year into its code
  6. Leap-year rule
  7. Century adjustment — any century, not just this one
  8. Full date — everything together, at speed

Inside the app

Correct back to year 1

Most calendar apps project today’s rules backwards. Day Genius uses the calendar people actually had: Britain and its colonies skipped 3–13 September 1752, and before that every fourth year was a leap year with no century exception. The engine is cross-checked against an independent algorithm across thousands of dates.

Why does Day Genius give a different weekday than my calendar app for old dates?

Because it is using the calendar people actually had. Britain and its colonies skipped 3–13 September 1752 when they switched from the Julian to the Gregorian calendar, and before that every fourth year was a leap year with no century exception. Day Genius applies those historical rules; most calendar apps project today’s rules backwards instead. See Reference › Before 1752 in the app.

Why won’t the date picker let me choose 7 September 1752?

That day never happened. Those eleven days were removed from the calendar, so the picker skips them rather than offering a date that did not exist.
